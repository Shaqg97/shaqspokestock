# Shaq's Poke Stock — Market Research & Inventory Operating System

Research date: 20 November 2026
Market focus: eBay Australia, Pokémon TCG singles and lots
Currency: AUD

## 1. What higher-volume sellers appear to do

This is a workflow and market-practice review, not an audit of private seller profits. Public listing counts, sold counts, advertised throughput and seller claims do not reveal acquisition cost, fees, labour, returns or net profit.

Patterns observed across seller guides, Australian market coverage, high-volume seller content and eBay guidance:

1. Separate cards by economic value and demand before listing. Individually list cards that have searchable demand; bundle commodity bulk where the listing and fulfilment effort would consume the margin.
2. Use real sold comparables, not the highest active asking price. Match exact set, collector number, language, finish/variant and condition.
3. Standardise the intake-to-listing pipeline: sort, scan front/back, identify, inspect condition, verify catalogue match, price, list, assign a physical SKU, store, then pick by SKU after sale.
4. Use real photos and clear condition disclosure. Show front and back; use close-ups for flaws and expensive cards.
5. Keep stock quantity and listing status synchronised. Out-of-stock cancellations and late dispatch can hurt seller performance.
6. Use repeatable postage policies, packaging standards and combined-postage settings. Offer tracking as an upgrade or default for higher-value orders.
7. Review sales and unsold listings regularly. Reprice or bundle stagnant low-value cards rather than allowing dead stock to grow indefinitely.
8. Maintain feedback and fast dispatch. Protect cards from movement, moisture, bends and surface damage.

A documented specialist workflow from Celeste TCG uses a Ricoh fi-8170, front/back scanning, human review, SKU-based storage and safe fulfilment; its published store had 1,686 live listings when documented. That is useful evidence of a repeatable workflow, not proof of its profit.
A separate Australian bulk-listing service advertises a scan-to-sold-comps-to-Seller-Hub-CSV pipeline and a throughput claim. Treat vendor throughput claims as marketing, not a guaranteed result.
A public Reddit discussion of a large seller reported 19,000 active listings and 650 sales in 90 days (about 3.4% of the active listing count over that period). It illustrates why listing count alone is a poor measure of business success; it is an anecdote, not an audited benchmark.

## 2. Shaq's Poke Stock listing strategy

Use a mixed strategy, with these as review bands rather than inflexible rules:

- A$20+ expected sale value: normally consider an individual listing, subject to demand, condition and sold comps.
- A$5–A$19.99: individual listing if sold demand is visible or the card is a recognisable character, playable card, popular artwork, vintage print or desirable variant; otherwise consider a themed bundle.
- Under A$5: normally bundle by meaningful theme, Pokémon/evolution line, set, playset, trainer/energy type or rarity. Do not create random mystery lots or claim a specific card is included unless it is pictured and supplied.
- True commodity bulk: sell as clearly counted lots (e.g. commons/uncommons, energies, reverse holos) or move through a bulk buyer. Pull valuable and playable cards first.
- High-value, vintage, graded and fragile/condition-sensitive cards: separate storage and tracked/insured shipping decision.

The bands are starting points. Use expected net profit and sold velocity to make the final choice. If a low-price individual listing is likely to earn less than the value of the time and materials needed to list and ship it, bundle it.

## 3. Physical inventory system — stable SKU locations

Do not alphabetically reshuffle the physical stock every time a new card arrives. That creates rework and makes picking slower. Keep the physical storage stable and let the digital inventory search by Pokémon, set, number, language, rarity and variant.

Use three storage classes:

- H = Hits / valuable singles. Store in a dedicated binder or protected high-value box. Location format: H01-P02-04 = High-value binder 01, page 02, pocket 04.
- S = Standard individually listed singles. Store in numbered card boxes in the order received. Location format: S01-0042 = Singles box 01, slot/card position 0042.
- L = Bundles and bulk lots. Store in labelled lot boxes/bags. Location format: L01-0012 = Lot box 01, lot 0012.

For a second storage unit, increment the box/binder number. Never reuse a SKU after an item sells; mark the inventory record sold and retain the old SKU for audit history.

For high-value items, use a dedicated location and a protective sleeve/holder appropriate to the card. For standard singles, sleeve each card and keep it in a stable numbered slot. Keep boxes dry, away from direct sunlight and temperature extremes.

## 4. Digital listing and inventory fields

Every record should include:
- Internal SKU / physical location (mandatory before publishing)
- Listing status: To identify, To price, Ready to list, Listed, Sold, Packed, Shipped, Returned, Archived
- Card name, set, collector number, language, rarity, finish/variant and condition
- Quantity and acquisition cost per card
- Active listing ID/URL, listing format and asking price
- Recent sold-comparable price, number of comparable sales, source and checked date
- Buyer-paid standard postage and actual standard postage cost
- Buyer-paid tracked postage and actual tracked postage cost
- Packaging cost, applicable fee assumptions, sale price, fees and realised net profit
- Intake date, listing date, sale date and dispatch date

Use exact set and number as the identity key. Never merge different language, holo/reverse-holo, promo, stamped, first-edition or other print variants into one inventory record.

## 5. Intake and listing workflow

1. Intake: count cards and record the purchase/lot cost. Allocate cost across cards consistently when needed.
2. Triage: separate hits, playable/searchable singles, standard bulk, damaged cards and non-Pokémon cards.
3. Scan: capture front and back; pair by the same scan ID or filename. Do not rely only on filenames where pairs are ambiguous.
4. Identify: AI proposes name/set/number/language/variant; human verifies exact print.
5. Condition: inspect front/back, edges and corners. Record conservative condition; photograph flaws.
6. Price: compare recent eBay Australia sold results for the exact print and condition. Record median/typical sold price and checked date, not just an active asking price.
7. Route: individual, themed bundle, counted bulk lot or hold.
8. SKU: assign a permanent storage location before listing.
9. List: use consistent title structure, clear real photos, condition details, buyer-paid postage and combined postage where appropriate.
10. Store: put the item in its exact SKU location immediately.
11. Fulfil: pick using SKU, verify card/variant/condition, pack securely, dispatch within handling time and record tracking.
12. Reconcile: record actual item revenue, postage collected, actual postage, fees, packaging and cost basis to calculate realised profit.

## 6. Suggested eBay title structure

[Pokémon/Card Name] [Set Name] [Card Number] [Rarity/Variant] [Language] [Condition]

Only include attributes that are verified and useful to buyers. Do not keyword-stuff unrelated Pokémon or sets.

For bundles:
[Theme/Character/Set] Pokémon TCG Card Lot [Card Count] [Language] [Holo/Non-Holo Mix]

State exact count, language, condition range and what is pictured/included.

## 7. Postage and profit rules

The buyer pays postage. Show buyer-paid postage separately from item price and from the actual carrier cost. Calculate the marketplace fee on the fee base applicable to the account and listing; do not assume that postage is excluded from fees. Set the fee rate and fixed fee in the workspace to the actual applicable account/category values before relying on profit estimates.

Use untracked letter services only when the packed item is eligible under current Australia Post requirements and eBay rules. Use tracked shipping for higher-value cards and any shipment where protection/evidence matters. Do not force a rigid toploader into a letter service if it makes the package ineligible. Use combined-postage discounts only when the actual incremental postage and packaging still leave acceptable margin.

Estimated net profit:
item revenue + buyer-paid postage - acquisition cost - packaging - actual postage - selling fees - other variable costs.

Realised net profit must use actual order-level fees and postage, not listing estimates. Exclude tax from the per-item estimate unless the business accounting model explicitly includes it.

## 8. Weekly operating review

- Check which listings sold and calculate actual net profit.
- Identify views/watchers but no sales; compare price and photos with sold competitors.
- Reprice stale singles; bundle low-demand low-value cards.
- Check stock status and quantities to prevent overselling.
- Review late dispatch, item-not-received and item-not-as-described metrics.
- Track sell-through by category: hits, playable singles, character/art singles, bundles and bulk.
- Buy more inventory only where the expected margin and sell-through justify it.

## 9. Research cautions

Seller revenue screenshots, active listing counts and advertised scan speeds are not proof of profit. Public data rarely exposes cost of goods, unsold stock, labour, fees, returns or tax. Use these examples as process benchmarks, then measure Shaq's Poke Stock using its own realised net profit and sell-through data.
